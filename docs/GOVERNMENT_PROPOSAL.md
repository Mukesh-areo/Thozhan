# Thozhan government pilot concept

We propose a bounded Chennai auto-mobility pilot to evaluate transparent fare calculation, driver economics, reliable pickups and accountable support. Thozhan is currently a working web demonstration. Production development, legal approval and operating arrangements are still required.

## Proposed request

Identify a nodal officer, clarify applicable transport and aggregator requirements, coordinate with relevant transport bodies and consider permission for a defined pilot. Any procurement, licence, PPP or subsidy is a separate decision; none is assumed.

## Pilot design

Start with a small service area and a proposed 50–150 verified auto drivers, recruited before consumer marketing. Agree operating hours, staffed incident handling, insurance responsibilities and a manual recovery plan. Measure results against a pre-agreed baseline and independently review before expansion.

## Public outcomes

Publish aggregate evidence of fare accuracy, completed requests, pickup times, accessibility, driver take-home earnings and complaint resolution. Offer Tamil-first usability and clear receipts. Integrate transit connections only with authorised partners and dependable supply.

## Commercial and ownership options

Distinguish a private operator, government software procurement and PPP in any final offer. Define who pays for development, support, maps, messaging, verification, insurance, hosting and customer acquisition. Specify IP ownership/licensing, data responsibilities, audit access, SLA, liability, change control, transition assistance and exit rights.

The proposed ₹10 fee applies after the first eligible completed trip per India-calendar day. It is a hypothesis to test. At 2,000 paying drivers and 25 paid days per month, gross subscription revenue is ₹5 lakh before costs and taxes; this is arithmetic, not a profitability forecast.

## Evidence required before submission

Company credentials, team responsibilities, budget quotations, tariff order and legal matrix, security/privacy assessment, driver consultation findings, pilot operating plan, service levels and evaluation protocol. The original plan's ₹37–85 lakh pilot estimate must be refreshed against actual scope and quotations and must not be described as full competitor-parity cost.

A government-ready proposal should avoid claims of endorsement, approved tariffs or guaranteed outcomes until documented.
