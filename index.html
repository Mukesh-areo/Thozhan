Proposal to the Government of Tamil Nadu

Fair fares for every ride. One honest meter for all of Tamil Nadu.

Thozhan is a citizen-led, Tamil-first mobility platform for autos, authorised bike services, cabs and parcel delivery. At its heart is a government-configurable digital auto meter. It works for app bookings and street hails, and gives the passenger and the driver the same itemised receipt.

What we ask for
Why it matters

Everyday travel should not start with an argument

Passengers, drivers and the Government all lose when fares are unclear and data is locked inside private platforms.

Fare disputes

Street-hail fares are often negotiated instead of metered, even when the Government has notified a tariff.

Unpredictable prices

App fares can rise with demand-based surge pricing that is not part of any approved tariff.

Commission on drivers

Percentage commissions reduce what drivers take home on every single trip.

No public data

Planners get little reliable, privacy-safe evidence about how people actually move.

The central feature

A digital auto meter the Government controls

The Government sets the tariff once; Thozhan applies it to every trip, booked or hailed on the street. It records the approved tariff, distance, waiting time and fare rules, and prints the same receipt for passenger and driver. Try it with the current notified auto tariff:

  • Works for app bookings and street-hail trips
  • Tariff changes are made centrally, effective from the date in the order
  • GPS distance measured on the phone and checked on the server
  • Passengers can report an overcharge from the receipt

For drivers

Keep what you earn

Thozhan proposes a flat ₹10 platform charge, only after the driver's first completed eligible trip of the day. No commission. No charge on days with no trips. Passengers pay the driver directly by cash or UPI.

Services and safeguards

Every service only with the right approval

Services

Auto (booked and street hail)Notified tariff
Bike taxi and scooterSubject to approval
CabSubject to approval
Parcel delivery (bike and auto)Subject to approval

Only autos would run in the first pilot. Each other service can be switched on centrally, only after the Government approves it and its fares.

Safeguards built in

  • Driver documents and vehicle verified before going online
  • Trip OTP and number-plate check before every ride
  • Parcel declaration and photos at pickup and delivery
  • Personal data minimised; passengers can download or delete their data
  • Complete audit trail of fares, fees and admin decisions
Proposed pilot

Start small, measure honestly, then decide

A bounded Chennai auto pilot, with results published against an agreed baseline before any expansion.

Phase 0

Approvals

Nodal officer, tariff and licence clarity, security review, driver consultation.

Phase 1

Closed pilot

50–150 verified auto drivers in one service area, with staffed support and incident handling.

Phase 2

Measure and publish

Fare accuracy, pickup times, driver take-home pay, complaints resolved, accessibility.

Phase 3

Expand if approved

More areas and services only after an independent review.

Government support needed

What we respectfully request

Thozhan cannot succeed as a private app alone. A fair public meter needs the Government's guidance and backing.

CUMTA

Guidance on the pilot area, integration with public transport, and use of aggregated mobility data.

Transport and Road Safety Department

Confirmation of how the notified auto tariff applies to the digital meter, aggregator requirements, and policy for bike and cab services.

Transport Commissionerate

A process to verify driver licences, permits and vehicle documents.

TNeGA

Hosting, data-protection and security-audit guidance, and possible e-governance integration.

Labour Welfare and Gig Workers Welfare Board

Linking drivers' earnings records to welfare registration and benefits.

Finance Department

Review of the ₹10 platform charge, revenue-share options and the pilot budget.

Police and emergency response

Connecting the SOS feature to 112 and women's helpline 181 workflows.

A nodal officer

One point of contact to coordinate departments and approve a defined pilot.

Transparency

Current limitations

  • Not yet in public service. The app has been tested on a test server and a real phone, not with live passengers.
  • Approvals pending. Bike, scooter, cab and parcel fares are proposals until approved.
  • Tariff details to confirm. Rounding and night-charge rules need confirmation from the Department.
  • Figures are scenarios. Revenue and savings numbers are planning arithmetic, not guaranteed outcomes.
  • Insurance would be offered only through a licensed insurer.
  • Before launch: security audit, production hosting, SMS registration and staffed support are still required.
